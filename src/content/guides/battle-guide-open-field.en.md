Open field combat happens on the map outside a structure battle and can involve one march or many
grouped marches. Commander skills, troop composition, equipment, technology, positioning and the
number of armies all affect the trade; a pairing rating alone does not predict the result.

## 1. Understanding Open Field

Open field severe wounds normally enter Hospitals, unlike several rally and structure contexts.
That makes retreat timing and Hospital capacity part of the decision, not permission to take every
available fight.

## 2. Before the Fight

- Match your commander pairing to your troop type — see [Commander Pairings](/pairings).
- Know your march's effective power relative to likely targets before engaging, not after.
- Agree on a rally point and retreat point with your group before moving out.

## 3. During the Fight

- Focus fire on priority targets rather than spreading damage across the enemy force.
- Watch for reinforcements arriving mid-fight — a fight that looks won can flip quickly.
- Adjust march speed and positioning rather than committing to a single line of approach.

## 4. After the Fight

Return to heal and rebuild before your next engagement. Fighting back-to-back without healing
capacity is one of the fastest ways to turn one loss into several.

## 5. Tips & Tricks

Communication and coordination with your alliance is the single biggest multiplier in open field —
a well-timed, well-communicated group with average commanders regularly beats a stronger but
uncoordinated one.

## 6. Common Mistakes

- Engaging without checking who else is nearby and able to reinforce the enemy.
- Using a commander pairing built for garrison or rally defense in an open field attack.
- Continuing to commit troops to a fight that is already lost instead of retreating early.
