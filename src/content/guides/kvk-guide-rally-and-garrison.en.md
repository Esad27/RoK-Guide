Rallying and garrisoning are two sides of the same coin during KvK: one is about concentrating
force to attack, the other is about concentrating force to hold.

## Rally Basics

A rally lets multiple players combine their troops into a single march led by a rally captain's
commander pairing. Because only the captain's commanders' skills apply to the whole rally, captain
selection matters more than any individual joiner's commanders.

## Garrison Basics

Garrisoning a city or fort reinforces its defense with your troops without exposing them the way an
open march would. This is the primary way an alliance holds a strongpoint against incoming rallies.

## Coordinating the Two

- Decide in advance who is rallying and who is garrisoning — trying to do both with the same troops
  at the same time usually means doing neither well.
- Keep a reserve garrison ready to reinforce a fort under rally threat rather than committing every
  available troop to offense.
- Communicate rally timers clearly; a rally that lands off-sync with the rest of the alliance's plan
  can get isolated and lost.

## Before troops are committed

Name the exact target, captain, commander pair, troop type, fill limit and launch time. City attacks,
alliance structures, passes and neutral objectives have different casualty rules. Reinforcing an
allied structure can create dead troops, while attacking cities or alliance buildings carries a
different severe-wound outcome from ordinary open field. Confirm current in-game rules and do not
fill by habit.
