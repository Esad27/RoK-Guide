March management is the discipline of turning several individually useful armies into one coherent
field lineup. More marches do not automatically create more value: every additional march needs troops,
equipment, armaments, commander investment and enough speed to stay with the group.

## 1. Build from one complete march outward

For F2P and low-spend accounts, one properly equipped march is usually more sustainable than several
underbuilt marches. Add another march only when it can survive the same battlefield and does not consume
the equipment or commander investment needed by the first.

1. Complete the primary commander pair and its required skill levels.
2. Equip the march with a coherent troop-type set.
3. Confirm armaments and formation support its battlefield role.
4. Add the second march without dismantling the first.
5. Repeat only while hospital and resource costs remain sustainable.

## 2. Give every march a role

A five-march lineup should combine durability, area damage, support and mobility. Five damage-focused
pairs can look powerful on paper but collapse when focused. Likewise, a very slow tank can separate from
the group and expose the faster marches.

Label each preset by role rather than only by commander name: frontline, area damage, support, finisher
or mobile response. This makes target calls and retreats easier during crowded fights.

## 3. Presets and deployment order

- Save troop type, commanders, equipment and formation together.
- Deploy the slowest march first so faster marches can form around it.
- Keep the same march order on screen to reduce selection mistakes.
- Check troop counts after healing; a preset can load with fewer troops than expected.
- Do not occupy every slot with combat when gathering or reinforcement is still required.

## 4. Keep the group together

Move to alliance markers before engaging. Select one target, concentrate damage, then return to the
group. If one march turns yellow or red earlier than the others, pull it back instead of allowing the
enemy to convert it into an easy elimination.

The practical limit is not the number of march slots your account has. It is the number of marches you
can control, equip and heal without breaking formation or exhausting your war economy.

## 5. Investment review

Before spending sculptures or crafting a second set, ask whether the upgrade improves a march that will
actually enter the field. Long-term value comes from completed roles and reusable equipment, not from
owning many partially built commander pairs.

## Pre-battle checklist

- Every commander pair has its intended skill state.
- Equipment and armaments are on the correct primary commander.
- March speeds are close enough to move as one group.
- Hospital capacity and healing resources are available.
- Rally, garrison and gathering slots are not being unintentionally consumed.
