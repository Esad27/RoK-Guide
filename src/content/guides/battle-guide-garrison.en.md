Garrisoning means stationing your troops inside a city or fort to reinforce its defense rather than
marching them out. It is the primary defensive counterpart to rallying.

## Why Garrison Instead of Defend in the Open

A garrisoned structure typically benefits from defensive bonuses that an open-field march does not
get, which is why holding a fort or city under rally pressure usually means filling it with garrison
troops rather than meeting the attack outside.

## Choosing a Garrison Commander Pairing

Garrison-appropriate pairings usually favor defensive skills and survivability over pure damage —
check a commander's [Best Uses](/commanders) tag on this site before assuming an offense-focused
pairing will hold up on defense.

## Coordinating Garrison Defense

- Fill available garrison capacity before an incoming rally lands, not after.
- Spread garrison responsibility across multiple defenders when possible rather than relying on one
  player's troops to hold an entire structure.
- Keep a reinforcement plan ready in case the first wave of an attack breaks through.

## Structure and casualty checks

City defense and alliance-structure defense are not interchangeable. Some skills mention your own
city or Watchtower and do not apply to every stronghold. Reinforcing an allied city or structure
can also convert part of severe wounds into dead troops. The captain should verify the exact skill
text, accepted troop type and target rules before calling reinforcements.
